Electronic Arts / Maxis — Competitive Research

Nine players, one ride-along, a new studio-wide method.

9Player ride-along interviews
60 minPer session, with a co-design exercise
Studio-wideNew methodology adopted beyond this study

The situation

EA's competitive analysis program looked at competitor titles the way most studios do: broad feature audits, comprehensive but rarely tied to a specific decision. Teams produced thorough reports that rarely changed what got built, because the research answered "what does the competition have" rather than "why do players actually choose to spend their time there."

I saw an opportunity in Roblox specifically. Tycoon-style games on the platform had a huge, dedicated player base, and understanding what made them work — or fall flat — could speak directly to questions Maxis was facing about player engagement and creative ownership.

What I did

I replaced the broad audit with a tighter, player-centered approach: nine 60-minute interviews with teens and young adults who already played tycoon games on Roblox. Each session followed a ride-along format — watching someone play a game they already knew, in their own words, rather than asking them to evaluate something cold. Sessions ended with a co-design exercise, asking players to imagine the settings, interactions, and pacing they'd want from a tycoon experience built for them.

The findings were specific enough to act on immediately. Players consistently disengaged from games built around walking between two points or stepping on buttons to progress — what one participant described as the difference between loading a song and playing the piano. What kept players engaged was customization, problem-solving, and a sense of authorship over their own space.

The outcome

The approach itself became the bigger win. EA shifted its competitive analysis program from broad product evaluations to this targeted, decision-driving framework — a methodology change that extended well past this one study and into how the studio approaches competitive research going forward.
