ELECTRONIC ARTS / MAXIS

How I helped Maxis design for a new generation of players

A research initiative that aligned a PC-first studio around a younger, mobile-first, multiplayer audience and changed how ideas were generated across development.

The Opportunity

I identified the knowledge gaps preventing the studio from building for its future audience

Project Rene wasn't being built for the traditional Sims audience. It targeted a younger generation that grew up with mobile devices, multiplayer games, and platforms like Roblox.

The studio's design experience reflected decades of building The Sims for longtime PC players, and product decisions carried that experience forward.

Ideas generated early in development did not consistently align with the motivations and expectations of this audience. Those gaps surfaced later through research and playtesting, creating rework and slowing the team's progress.

You could see it in the game itself, right down to the denim. The characters wore skinny jeans, because that is what the people building them wore. The audience the studio was chasing had long since moved on to baggy.

The studio needed a shared, applied understanding of this audience to guide decisions during ideation and development. I set out to build that understanding and embed it into how the studio made product decisions.

Building a Shared Understanding

I maintained a continuous synthesis of audience evidence across many inputs

No single study could answer these questions.

I combined consumer research, motivational profiling, exploratory interviews, fashion and expression research, Roblox ride-alongs, comparative usability studies, genre deep dives, workshops, and observational research.

Each input answered a different question and updated one continuously maintained understanding of the audience: how this generation played, communicated, formed friendships, expressed identity, and decided which games deserved their attention.

Leadership used this work to define motivational profiles that became a shared language across product, design, and research.

Translating Insight Into Decisions

I communicated the research at every level of the organization

Different teams needed different answers.

Leadership

Used audience patterns to steer product direction and investment decisions.

Developers

Used observed player behavior to generate and pressure-test ideas.

Designers

Used applied principles to shape systems, mechanics, and features.

I translated the same body of research across those different levels, helping leadership steer the product while giving individual teams confidence in the decisions they were making every day.

Bringing the Studio Into the Audience's World

I helped developers learn from the games this audience already loved

One question kept surfacing. If these players spend thousands of hours in Roblox, what can we learn from it?

I facilitated collaborative Roblox sessions where developers experienced the platform together, documented observations, surfaced questions, and discussed what made those experiences compelling.

Those conversations led to a new research approach.

I developed ride-along studies where Roblox players shared their screens while playing the experiences they already loved. Combined with comparative studies across games like Fortnite, Minecraft, and Roblox, the work informed multiplayer systems, controls, progression, cosmetics, social features, and the broader product strategy.

Roblox stopped being viewed as a competitor. It became one of the studio's richest sources of inspiration for understanding the audience.

"It isn't about making a higher fidelity version of Roblox. In fact their players don't really care about that much. It's about understanding the motivations, mental models, and behaviors of an audience that grew up playing Roblox so that we can build an exemplary experience that delights and retains that audience."

Scaling the Approach

Leadership expanded the model across the studio

As the work gained momentum, leadership reorganized development pods around genres relevant to the new audience and invested in exploratory research across those spaces.

Teams increasingly looked to player behavior before generating ideas, allowing development to begin from evidence instead of assumption.

Impact

The studio changed how it designed for a new generation of players

At the beginning of this work, product decisions were largely informed by assumptions carried forward from previous Sims titles.

By the end, teams shared a common understanding of the audience they were building for and used that understanding to guide decisions across multiplayer, controls, progression, cosmetics, art direction, and social play.

Developers began inviting me into standups to explore Roblox together. Leadership expanded investment in exploratory research. Teams became increasingly curious about understanding players before deciding what to build.

Somewhere along the way, the skinny jeans became baggy.

It wasn't really about denim. It was evidence that the studio had started designing for the players it wanted to win.
