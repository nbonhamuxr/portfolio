HOW I LEAD

Leadership Style

I've led research across gaming studios, academic publishing, and enterprise technology. These are the principles that show up regardless of the context.

Nurturing

01

I want the people around me to feel supported, seen, and capable of more than they thought. That shows up in how I give feedback, how I hold space for uncertainty, and how I celebrate the success of others.

Leaning In with Curiosity

02

My instinct when something's unclear isn't to fill the gap with assumptions — it's to ask more questions. I find ambiguity genuinely interesting. Curiosity is contagious in a room: when a researcher approaches a problem with open, genuine interest, the people around them tend to start doing the same.

Building Alignment

03

I'm often described as the person in the room who gets everyone speaking the same language. I design scoping, synthesis, and co-working workshops so teams reach insight and know how to act on that insight together.

Transparency

04

I'd rather name what I don't know than project confidence I haven't earned. I share my thinking early and often, including the parts that are still rough. That openness makes everyone around me more willing to participate throughout the research process.

Mentoring & Coaching

05

I've mentored graduate students at the University of Michigan and served as a formal mentor at JSTOR, where my mentee was promoted from Associate User Researcher to User Researcher. Helping someone grow into their own voice is some of the most satisfying work I do.

Authoritative, Not Authoritarian

06

I take an evidence-informed approach to everything I do. I'm comfortable saying "I don't know — but we'll figure it out," and I mean it. I take deep pride in my craft and in the work I put into the world, but I don't need to announce it. Expertise shows in the work.
