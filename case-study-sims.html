Electronic Arts / Maxis — Project Rene

Aligning a studio around who a mobile-first Sims player actually is.

The situation

Project Rene is a mobile-first, multiplayer concept in The Sims franchise, which meant the team was designing for two different audiences at once: longtime Sims fans with deeply held expectations, and an entirely new population of mobile-first, new-to-franchise players. The studio's existing development instincts were shaped by years of building for the first group. Without a clear, shared picture of the second, decisions about UI, controls, and pacing risked optimizing for the wrong player.

What I did

I combined end-to-end quantitative and qualitative research — covering UI, gameplay, controls, visual style, fashion, and multiplayer frameworks — to build a research-driven narrative about who this new player actually was: their mental models, their expectations, and what they valued differently from a longtime fan.

The harder part was making that narrative stick across a full studio, not just a single team. I worked inside the existing agile rhythm — sprint planning, retrospectives, requirement sessions — to keep research and development aligned as the work evolved, and built a participatory research culture around it: cross-team scoping workshops, studio-wide playtest viewing sessions, and multi-channel insight sharing through 1:1s, show-and-tells, Slack, and studio-wide all-hands. I also coached teams directly on reading their own behavioral data in BigQuery and Looker, so the insight didn't depend on me being in the room.

The outcome

The studio's development culture shifted to genuinely account for mobile-first, new-to-franchise players, not as an afterthought layered onto the existing design, but as a population shaping decisions from the start. The controls work alone raised the ease-of-use score by 55%, driven by directly addressing the usability issues and expectations specific to mobile-first players.
